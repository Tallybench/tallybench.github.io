Markup vs margin: what's the difference?

Both describe your profit as a percentage. The difference is what you divide by, and getting it wrong is an easy way to under-price a job.

The short version

  • Markup = profit ÷ cost. It answers: "how much did I add on top of what it cost me?"
  • Margin = profit ÷ selling price. It answers: "how much of every sales dollar is profit?"

Take an item that costs you $80 and sells for $100. Profit is $20. Markup is 20 ÷ 80 = 25%. Margin is 20 ÷ 100 = 20%. Same sale, two numbers. Margin is always the smaller one (for any positive profit), and margin can never reach 100%, while markup can be any size.

The mistake that costs people money

Say you want a 30% margin and your supplier charges you $70. If you add 30% to your cost, you get $91. Your real margin is (91 − 70) ÷ 91 = 23.1%, not 30%. To really earn a 30% margin, divide the cost by (1 − 0.30): $70 ÷ 0.70 = $100. On that price your profit is $30, which is 30% of $100, and a markup of about 42.9%.

Rule of thumb: if you're thinking in margin, divide; if you're thinking in markup, multiply.

Formulas

You knowSelling price
Cost and markup %cost × (1 + markup)
Cost and margin %cost ÷ (1 − margin)
Cost and pricemarkup = (price − cost) ÷ cost
margin = (price − cost) ÷ price

To convert directly: margin = markup ÷ (1 + markup) and markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin). A 50% markup is a 33.3% margin. A 100% markup (doubling your cost) is a 50% margin.

Discounts hurt more than they look

Because margin is measured against price, a discount reduces it faster than you'd expect. With a cost of $70 and a $100 price, your margin is 30%. Give a 10% discount and the price falls to $90, so profit drops from $30 to $20 and margin falls to 22.2%. You gave up 10% of the price but a third of your profit.

Margin isn't the same as take-home

Both numbers here are gross: they only account for the cost of the item or job itself. Rent, software, insurance, your own time and taxes still come out of that profit. When you set prices, make sure the gross margin is big enough to cover those overheads with something left over.
